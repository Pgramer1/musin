## 1. Purpose

RQ1 asks which artists connect otherwise separate genres. PRD §5 answers with two ingredients:

- **genre diversity:** how evenly an artist's collaboration weight spreads over its neighbours' genres;
- **betweenness:** whether the artist sits on paths between otherwise separate parts of the graph.

Their product is the **bridge score**. F4.3 also builds the genre × genre matrix of who collaborates with whom, and draws ego networks of the top bridges for the report.

## 2. What was built

- **`python -m musin.analytics.bridges`.** It runs on the graph named by `loaded_graph.json`: Neo4j for betweenness and write-back, and the **same graph's Parquet** (the marker's `source`) for diversity and the matrix. It needs F4.1's `betweenness` on that graph and fails otherwise.
- **Spark functions:** `collab_pairs` (D-017 pairs), `neighbour_genres`, `diversity`, `genre_matrix`. **Pandas helpers:** `percentile_rank` (D-038), `square_matrix`, `genre_profile`.
- **`bridge_figures.py`:** a row-normalised genre heatmap (sequential blue ramp) and radial ego networks (D-038).
- **`neo4j/07_bridges.cypher`:** read betweenness, write `genreDiversity`/`bridgeScore`, count the write-back.
- **Config keys:** `analysis.ego_figures`, `analysis.ego_max_neighbours` (D-038).
- **Image change:** `fonts-noto-cjk` in the Spark image, so CJK artist names render in figures.
- **9 unit tests:** pair merging across types and directions, the diversity formula against a hand-computed value, the threshold and zero diversity, the symmetric matrix without unlabelled pairs, the zero-filled square matrix, mid-rank percentiles, genre profiles with ties, the ego layout (cap, grouping, palette slots, order), and that the Cypher statements exist.

## 3. How it works

1. **Pairs** (Spark): sum the weight of every edge type per unordered artist pair. That is COLLAB (D-017), whose weight is shared recordings (D-028).
2. **Neighbour genres:** for each artist, sum the pair weight to labelled neighbours per neighbour primary genre. Unlabelled neighbours are left out (PRD §5).
3. **Diversity:** `D_i = 1 − Σ_g (w_ig / W_i)²` over those genre weights. It is defined only with ≥ `analysis.min_labelled_neighbours` (5) labelled neighbours, otherwise null.
4. **Genre matrix:** every pair with both ends labelled adds its weight to the cell (genre a, genre b), unordered, so A–B and B–A meet in one cell. Then expand it to a symmetric table with zeros over the primary genres present in the graph: 14 × 14 on the main graph, where no artist has primary genre classical (D-039), and 15 × 15 on the `classical` variant.
5. **Betweenness percentile** (Neo4j → pandas): read `betweenness` for **all** artists (PRD §5: the percentile is over the whole loaded graph). Fail if any artist lacks it, or if Neo4j and Parquet disagree on the artist count. Then compute (below + ½ equal) / N (D-038).
6. **Bridge score:** `B_i = D_i × pct_i` where `D_i` is defined. Rank by `B` descending, ties by `artistId`.
7. **Write back** `genreDiversity` and `bridgeScore` in batches of 50,000 and check the counts. Then check that the loaded graph is unchanged.
8. **Top table:** `analysis.top_k_bridges` (25) rows, with each artist's three largest neighbour genres ("rock 45%, jazz 30%, …"). Ego figures for the top `analysis.ego_figures` (3) bridges; a heatmap of the matrix.

**Design choices and alternatives:**

- **Spark for diversity, Neo4j for betweenness.** Diversity is an aggregation over 3.1 M edges (3,078,519 on the main graph), a Spark job on the same Parquet that was imported. Betweenness only exists in Neo4j (F4.1). The alternative, aggregating in Cypher, would group ~6 M directed rows inside the 8 GiB heap that also holds the database. Reading the Parquet path from `loaded_graph.json` guarantees the two halves describe the same graph, and an artist-count check confirms it.
- **The mid-rank percentile** (D-038). On the sample, 73 % of artists have betweenness 0, and "≤" or "<" conventions would put them all at 0.73 or at 0. The mid-rank gives them one value that doesn't depend on tie order.
- **Undefined stays absent.** Artists below the neighbour threshold get no `genreDiversity`/`bridgeScore` property rather than 0. A 0 would claim "single-genre collaborator", which isn't known.
- **The heatmap is row-normalised.** Raw weights are dominated by the biggest genres (jazz, electronic and hip hop on the main graph; classical on the `classical` variant). Row shares answer "where does genre X's collaboration go?" The CSV keeps raw weights, which are the PRD matrix.
- **Radial ego networks, not force layouts.** No graph-layout library is in the image (`networkx` is not a dependency). A radial layout grouped by genre shows the one thing the figure must show, a bridge's neighbours spread across genres, without layout noise.
- **Rejected:** Shannon entropy instead of Gini–Simpson diversity (not the PRD metric), and weighting the bridge score by unweighted neighbour counts (the PRD uses collaboration weight).

## 6. How to run

```bash
docker compose exec spark python -m musin.analytics.centrality --config config/pipeline.yaml   # F4.1 first, on the loaded graph
docker compose exec spark python -m musin.analytics.bridges --config config/pipeline.yaml
```

Runtime on the reference machine (2026-10-20 re-run): full graph **50 s** (Character variant 58 s, classical variant 54 s), sample 29 s. On the full graph, Spark runs with a **4 GB driver** (`docker compose exec -e SPARK_DRIVER_MEMORY=4g spark …`): F4.3 needs Spark and Neo4j at the same time, and the default 12 GB driver plus Neo4j's 13 GB exceeds WSL's 18 GB. A full Spark job with Neo4j running was OOM-killed on 2026-10-12. The driver size is a per-machine setting (`.env`) and doesn't change results.

## 8. Known issues and limitations

- **The bridge score is close to the diversity score** (D-038). Artists with ≥ 5 labelled neighbours are well connected, so their betweenness percentile is near 1. The ranking is therefore mostly a diversity ranking. Betweenness down-weights only the periphery.
- **Diversity is noisy near the threshold.** Some top bridges have few labelled neighbours (Féloche 13 on the main graph; Christian Lorenz 7 on the `classical` variant). One more collaborator in another genre moves `D` a lot. `labelled_neighbours` is in the top table so a reader can see it.
- **Labelled neighbours only.** The genre spread of an artist whose collaborators are mostly untagged rests on the tagged few.
- **One primary genre per artist** (PRD §5). An artist whose neighbours span genres because the *neighbours* are multi-genre still counts each neighbour once, by its primary genre.
- **Betweenness is sampled** (10,000 sources) and unweighted (F4.1). Percentiles are robust to sampling; exact values are not.
- **The sampling error carries into the bridge list.** Re-running on identical graph content moved raw betweenness by a median of 12 % for the top bridges and gave a top-25 Jaccard of only 0.92 (§7). Ranks within the top 25, and membership near rank 25, are not stable; differences between graphs smaller than that (such as the Character variant's 0.85) can't be separated from noise. A larger sample would reduce this; F4.1's betweenness is already most of P4's runtime.
- **Classical shapes the list's lower half** (D-039). The main graph leaves classical out; with it, the top-25 Jaccard drops to 0.61 (§7). The report should say which graph a bridge list comes from.
- **Record labels credited as artists** (Putumayo, Vineyard Music) reach the top lists. They are not on `config/special_artists.csv`, and the G1 decisions (D-039, D-040) did not add them.
- **Weight-based diversity under-rates artists with a big home genre** (Quincy Jones, Herbie Hancock; Yo‐Yo Ma on the provisional graph, now only in the `classical` variant; F4.6 §7.1).
