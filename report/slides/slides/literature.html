Our step

The complete open MusicBrainz export, not a crawl · typed credits: billed, featured, producer, performer · a bridge score that measures genre bridging directly

Methods from: Louvain (Blondel et al. 2008) · PageRank (Page et al. 1999) · betweenness (Freeman 1977; Brandes 2001) · NMI/AMI (Vinh et al. 2010)

About 45 seconds. Collaboration networks are a classic object of network science, starting with scientific co-authorship (Newman 2001). For music, Gleiser and Danon studied jazz musicians who played in the same bands and found communities that follow recording location and racial segregation. Park and colleagues built a network of contemporary popular musicians from an online music guide. Bryan and Wang ranked influence in a sampling network from WhoSampled and found funk, soul and disco behind hip hop, R&B and electronic music. These studies work with curated or crawled data at the scale of thousands of artists. Our step: the complete open MusicBrainz export, about a million artists, with typed credits including producers and performers, and a bridge score that measures genre bridging directly. Our methods build on Louvain, PageRank, betweenness and NMI/AMI. Full references are in the report.
